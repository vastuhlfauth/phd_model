# 12. Congestion extension (later phase)

- **A BPR function on the reduced network.** Congestion is modelled on the network that is actually searched, the grid edges and trunk links, not on OSM ways. Each edge e of edge group k has a travel time that grows with its flow (Bureau of Public Roads 1964):

```text
t_e(q_e) = t0_e · [ 1 + α_k · (q_e / c_e)^β_k ]
```

  where t0_e is the free-flow time of the edge (section 8.4), q_e its car flow in the peak hour, c_e its capacity, and α_k, β_k are calibrated per group: trunk links; grid edges by dominant road class and urban context. The Akçelik (1991) and conical functions are kept as alternatives to compare.
- **Capacity of an edge.** Trunk links keep the capacity of their lanes and class. A grid edge stands for several roads, so its capacity is not physical; two definitions are tested in the pilot: (a) the capacity of the bottleneck on the edge's own path (lanes × capacity per lane by class); (b) the capacity of all car roads crossing the boundary between the two cells (a screenline), which is direct for the orthogonal edges of the 8-neighbour graph (section 8.3). A scale factor per edge group is calibrated with α and β. Capacities are stored on every edge from the start, since scenarios edit them (section 11.4).
- **Demand.** Peak-hour car O-D flows between 1 km cells, from the distribution and mode-share models. Until they exist, MOBPRO car commuting flows between communes, allocated to cells with the origins and job keys and scaled to the peak hour [TO CONFIRM: peak-hour factor and share of non-commuting trips, from EMP 2019 and the EMC² surveys].
- **Assignment.** Within a period, static user-equilibrium assignment (bi-conjugate Frank-Wolfe) of the peak-hour demand on the reduced network with the calibrated functions; between periods (years, scenario epochs), the congested costs of one period feed the demand models of the next [TO CONFIRM: equilibrium within each period, or the out-of-equilibrium loop of version 9, in which flows at t set speeds at t+1 without a fixed point]. Candidate engine: AequilibraE (section 14.6), compared with an implementation on the CSR graph. Assignment is exact on the trunk layer and approximate on the grid.
- **Calibration data: the existing Google pipeline.** The current scripts (section 14.4) query the Google Routes API (`computeRoutes`) between the representative points of 200 m cells, for a sample stratified by modelled duration (100 O-D pairs per mode by default), at fixed departure times (07:00 and 08:20 on a Wednesday), with traffic-aware routing for cars. They request only the duration and distance of each route. For the congestion phase three more fields are needed: `staticDuration` (the duration without traffic), whose ratio to `duration` gives the congestion index of the route; the route `polyline`, to split routes into road sections and grid edges; and, for cars, the traffic on the polyline (`extraComputations: TRAFFIC_ON_POLYLINE`), which marks each interval as normal, slow or jammed [TO CONFIRM: pricing tier of these fields]. The terms of the Google Maps Platform on storing results must be checked before collecting data.
- **Calibration.** α_k, β_k and the capacity scale factors are chosen to minimise the error between modelled and Google peak car times on the sampled O-D pairs (free-flow times against `staticDuration`, congested times against `duration`), after matching each address to its 200 m and 1 km cells and removing the model's terminal legs. The polylines, split by grid edge, give an edge-level check: the observed congestion index of each edge against its modelled flow-to-capacity ratio. Traffic counts help separate capacity from α [TO CONFIRM: national and departmental count data].
- **Effects:** driving time; driving energy through the congestion penalty of section 9.6; parking search; buses in mixed traffic; bike stops (section 9.8).
- **Cost:** each equilibrium repeats all-or-nothing loadings of the national peak demand on the reduced network, about three times cheaper with 8 neighbours than with 24; the runtime per period is measured in the pilot. Between full assignments, path-fixed updates with the bounds of section 11.5 can be used.
