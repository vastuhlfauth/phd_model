# 15. Risks and open points

- **Rebuilt PT supply.** Both states use 2026 timetables: lines closed before 2026 are missing (8 known), frequencies are those of 2026, and changes outside trams depend on the completeness of the openings list; ordinary bus routes are those of 2026. OSM route relations are too incomplete for buses and rail to support an automatic rule (section 5.6).
- **BPE.** The retained groups are defined in two nomenclatures (2021 and 2023), and BPE 2025 stands for the 2023 state, two years later.
- **Fichiers fonciers.** Restricted access; public premises poorly described (hence OSM for the public sector); `cconac` of medium quality; variables that change between millésimes.
- **Origins imputation.** Large natural-level cells in rural areas leave most of the location to the keys; the validation by cell size measures this, and the 2023 Filosofi-like data rest on a model.
- **Time per mode.** f_m is held fixed in scenarios but drifted between 2008 and 2019 (car driver ×1.066, car passenger ×1.129, train ×1.082), mostly through longer and slower commuting trips that the literature and the macro speed data do not explain; stability is shown within ±15%, not ±10%. With each mode's 2008 distribution, the 2019 mean per person is underestimated by about 7%, an error that long-horizon scenarios carry (drift factor δ_m, section 10.4). Coarser rounding of declared durations in 2019 bounds part of the car drift (0 to 3.1 of 4.0 min). The estimates rest on EMP 2019, a pre-COVID survey, and on ENTD 2008, which select the described day differently (section 9.9). Over longer periods the constant is not tested: from 1982 to 2008 total time rose by only 3% while motorisation moved many trips from walking to the car, which fixed f_m would turn into a rise; the earlier surveys exist only as published aggregates (section 10.4). The total-time effect of large modal shifts is therefore the model's least tested output.
- **Mobility model.** The allocation assumes that persons are ordered within f_m by the time their activities require, with a correlation ρ_m calibrated on survey groups or on the EMC² surveys; whether required time reproduces the flat profile of motorised modes and the falling profile of walking across density is not yet tested. The public EMP 2019 and ENTD 2008 tables do not give the residence commune, so the accessibility terms of the mode-use model rest on the EMC² surveys and MOBPRO, or on restricted access (section 10.4).
- **Energy.** Two rate sets differ by about a third for walking; MET-to-kJ conversions carry about ±20%; energy depends strongly on walking legs, which are taken from declared values (section 9.9).
- **Declared trips.** Survey distances and durations contain implausible values, and a single trip with a large weight can move a group mean: one ENTD 2008 bike day reached 1.3 million kJ before correction. Speeds are checked for walking, cycling and car trips, not yet for public transport and train, and no mode has a duration check, which matters more now that time is the reference (section 9.9). EMP 2019 describes only days on which the respondent went out, while ENTD 2008 describes the weekday before the visit whatever happened on it: the surveys are compared on days with trips, and trips per person and day from EMP 2019 need the days without going out of the previous week (`IMMODEP`).
- **Congestion and crowding coefficients.** Physiological evidence for stress-related energy is thin; these coefficients are parameters with sensitivity ranges.
- **Congestion on a reduced network.** A grid edge stands for several roads, so its capacity is not physical; the BPR calibration absorbs the difference, but capacity and the α parameter are hard to separate without traffic counts.
- **Coarser grid.** With 8 neighbours the directional bias reaches 8.2% for straight trips; the pilot measures whether a correction factor is enough.
- **Storage.** Opportunity curves at 200 m and full car tables are large; the pilot sets bins, bands and what is kept.
- **PT computing time** from 2.3 million origins; the fast variant must be validated before national runs.
- **Overture schema changes and retention.** POI queries need versioned mappings and tests; releases disappear after 60 days.
- **Census pooling.** Census years pool five collections, so changes between 2021 and 2023 are smoothed.
- **OSM change between snapshots** mixes real infrastructure changes and mapping improvements; infrastructure edits for the back-test must be curated.
- **Foreign regions.** Only jobs and nature are covered abroad; jobs rest on Overture.
- **Approximations of the engine.** Level 1 updates, bounds and the decomposition by new elements are checked against full runs on samples and in the back-test.
